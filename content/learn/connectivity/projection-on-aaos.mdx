The foundations topic separated Android Auto from Android Automotive OS. Now the
case that muddles them again: an AAOS vehicle that **also** supports Android Auto.

Both at once. On the same screen.

## Why this exists at all

<Plain>
If the car already runs Android, why would anyone want to project a phone onto
it?

Because the two hold different things. Your phone has your accounts, your
messages, your recently played, your podcast progress and the apps you actually
subscribe to. The car has none of that unless you have signed in to each app on
the car — which people frequently have not.

<T>Projection</T> is the "I just want my phone's stuff" path, and it is also the
answer for a driver whose phone is an iPhone in a car running Android.
</Plain>

<Analogy title="Like a smart TV with an HDMI input">
The TV runs its own apps. It also has an HDMI port for a laptop.

Both work. They are different things. Nobody finds this confusing about
televisions, and it is exactly the same arrangement here — the car has its own
Android *and* an input for someone else's.
</Analogy>

## What it looks like architecturally

The projected session is, from AAOS's point of view, **just another app**.

```text title="The layering"
AAOS (the vehicle's own Android)
 ├── native media app
 ├── native navigation
 ├── settings
 └── projection app       ← hosts the phone session
      └── the phone's UI, streamed in as pixels
```

<Plain>
The projection app receives a video stream from the phone and displays it,
forwarding touches back. Everything outside that app's window — the system bars,
the climate panel — is still the car's own Android.

This is why a projected session cannot read vehicle data. It is a picture from
another device, inside a window. The phone is no closer to the CAN bus than it
was in the foundations topic.
</Plain>

## The handover rules

Two Androids competing for one screen and one set of speakers needs arbitration,
and getting it wrong is very visible to the driver.

**Audio focus is the mechanism.** The projection app requests focus like any
other app. When the phone plays music, the projection app holds media focus and
the car's own media app pauses.

**Vehicle functions always win.** A reversing camera, a collision warning, a
climate control interaction interrupts projection immediately. The car's own
safety and vehicle-control surfaces are never subordinate to a projected session.

**Navigation is the awkward one.**

<Scenario title="Two navigation systems, one cluster">
The driver starts navigation on their phone through projection. The car's own
navigation is idle.

Which one should send turn-by-turn instructions to the instrument cluster?

Most programmes give the cluster to whichever navigation source is *active*, with
the projected one able to claim it. But the cluster is often driven by a separate
safety-rated system expecting a specific message format, so the projection app
has to translate the phone's guidance into that format.

Whether that works is a per-programme integration detail. It is the single most
common projection complaint: "my phone navigation does not show in the
instrument cluster."
</Scenario>

## The wireless path

Wireless projection uses a direct Wi-Fi link between phone and head unit,
established over Bluetooth.

```text title="How a wireless session starts"
1. Phone and head unit are paired over Bluetooth
2. Head unit uses BLE/Bluetooth to hand the phone Wi-Fi credentials
3. Phone joins a direct Wi-Fi link with the head unit
4. Video and audio stream over that link
```

<Plain>
Worth knowing: **this Wi-Fi link is separate from the vehicle's internet
connection.** A car can be projecting wirelessly while completely offline, and it
can be online with no projection running. They use different radios or different
channels, and confusing them makes connectivity bugs very hard to reason about.

It also means projection consumes Wi-Fi radio capacity, which on a single-radio
head unit can compete with a passenger hotspot.
</Plain>

## What Android exposes

```kotlin title="CarProjectionManager, for a projection host app"
val projection = car.getCarManager(Car.PROJECTION_SERVICE) as CarProjectionManager

projection.registerProjectionRunner(serviceIntent)

// Ask the platform to start the Wi-Fi access point for a wireless session
projection.startProjectionAccessPoint(callback)
```

This needs `android.car.permission.CAR_PROJECTION` — signature-level, because a
projection host takes over the screen and audio. It is a system app, not
something a third party ships.

```bash title="Inspecting projection state"
adb shell dumpsys car_service --services CarProjectionService
adb shell dumpsys media_session | grep -i projection
adb shell dumpsys wifi | grep -i -A5 'SoftAp\|p2p'
```

## What this means if you build apps

<Plain>
Mostly: **you will be running in one world or the other, and you should know
which.**

If your app is installed on the vehicle, you are a native AAOS app with vehicle
data access, subject to the platform's distraction rules.

If your app is on the phone and appears through projection, you are an Android
Auto app — no vehicle data, a different SDK, a different review process, and a
completely different release cadence.

The same product may need both, and they are two builds against two SDKs, not one
build with a flag. Scoping a feature without knowing which one it targets is how
teams discover late that half of it is impossible.
</Plain>

## Common problems

| Symptom | Usual cause |
| --- | --- |
| Projection will not start | Bluetooth pairing incomplete; Wi-Fi handover failed |
| Audio comes out of the wrong place | Projection app's audio context misconfigured |
| Car media does not resume after | Projection did not abandon audio focus |
| Phone navigation absent from cluster | Cluster message translation not implemented |
| Wireless session drops | Wi-Fi radio contention with hotspot or scanning |
| Works with one phone, not another | Phone-side implementation differences — build a phone matrix |

<Recap>
- An AAOS vehicle can **also host projection**. They are different things
  coexisting, not versions of each other.
- A projected session is **just another app** to AAOS, and **cannot read vehicle
  data**.
- **Audio focus arbitrates** between the two; vehicle functions always take
  priority.
- **Navigation to the cluster** is the hardest integration point and the most
  common complaint.
- The **wireless projection link is separate** from the vehicle's internet
  connection.
- Building for AAOS and building for projection are **two different products** —
  decide which during scoping.
</Recap>
