It's called CarService — and if you've followed this series, you've already seen its name pop up twice: VHAL feeds it decoded signals, and it's what actually turns those signals into something an app can use.

Think of it the same way you'd think of ActivityManagerService or WindowManagerService in regular Android — except instead of managing activities or windows, CarService manages "car." It runs as a privileged system service inside system_server, and it's the only thing in AAOS allowed to talk directly to VHAL. Every app goes through it instead — never straight to hardware, always through a permission-gated Binder call.
It doesn't do this through one giant API. It's split into focused managers, each owning one slice of the vehicle:
→ CarPropertyManager — HVAC, seats, windows, lights
→ CarUserManager — driver profiles, fast account switching
→ CarAudioManager — multi-zone audio routing (nav vs. media vs. calls, at the same time)
→ CarProjectionManager — the same component that hosts Android Auto sessions from Day 1
→ CarWatchdogManager — flags apps that hang or overrun resources, so one bad app can't freeze the cluster

This is why AAOS feels consistent across completely different cars: every app is coded against the same Car API, regardless of what's wired underneath.
