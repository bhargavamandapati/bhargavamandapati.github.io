Most people (even some engineers) mix these up. Here's the actual difference:

Android Auto = your phone's screen, mirrored.

It's an app. Your phone does all the work — maps, music, calls — and just projects the interface onto the car's display. Unplug the phone, the screen goes blank.

Android Automotive OS (AAOS) = the car's actual operating system.

No phone required. AAOS runs natively on the vehicle's hardware, directly powering the infotainment, and in many builds, the instrument cluster too. It talks to the car itself — engine, HVAC, sensors — through a layer called VHAL (Vehicle HAL), which passes real vehicle signals into apps like climate control, range estimates, and diagnostics.

Think of it this way:

Android Auto is a guest borrowing the screen.

AAOS is the resident that owns the house.

This distinction matters more every year — GM, Ford, and several other automakers have already dropped Android Auto/CarPlay support in favor of a fully native AAOS experience. The car itself is becoming the computer.
