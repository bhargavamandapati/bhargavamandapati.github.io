Here's the problem it solves: your car's real world runs on raw electrical signals — CAN bus frames, voltage levels, sensor pulses. Android has zero understanding of any of that. It only speaks typed, clean data.

VHAL (Vehicle Hardware Abstraction Layer) sits exactly in between. It's the translator.

Example:
your climate sensor doesn't send "22°C." It sends a raw CAN frame like ID 0x3B2 → 4F 12 FF 00. VHAL is the piece that decodes that frame into a clean, named Android property: HVAC_TEMPERATURE_SET = 22.0. From there, CarService exposes it through one Car API, and any app — climate control, instrument cluster, a third-party dashboard — just reads a variable instead of a wire.

This is also why the exact same AAOS app can run in a Ford, a GM, and a Stellantis vehicle, even though the wiring, sensors, and ECUs underneath are completely different. Each OEM writes their own VHAL implementation for their hardware. Everything above it — CarService, the Car API, every app — stays identical.

One clean abstraction layer. Any car.
